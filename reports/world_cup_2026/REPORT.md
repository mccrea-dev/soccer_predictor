# 2026 World Cup: predictions vs. results

Generated by `python soccer.py worldcup`. Every forecast below was made by models trained only on matches played before that match day. Results are the score at the end of play; a knockout game decided on penalties counts as a draw.

## Headline

- Result (home / draw / away) called correctly in **70 of 104 matches (67.3%)**, 95% bootstrap interval 58-76%.
- Over/Under 2.5 goals called correctly in **61 of 104 (58.7%)**.
- Most likely scoreline was exactly right in 11 matches (10.6%).
- Average probability given to the outcome that actually happened: 49.4% (a coin-flip across three outcomes would be 33.3%).

## Draws

- 24 of 104 matches (23%) were level at the end of play; the model's average draw probability was 23%.
- The model made a draw its top pick 1 time(s). Draws are rarely the single most likely outcome, so nearly every draw is a guaranteed miss on accuracy. Excluding draws, the model picked the winner in 87.5% of 80 decisive matches.

## Live tracker: model vs. the betting market

During the tournament I logged the model's pick next to the de-vigged market favourite for 34 matches I had odds snapshots for.

- Logged model pick correct: 30/34 (88%)
- Market favourite correct: 27/34 (79%)
- Day-by-day replay pick on the same matches: 27/34 (79%)
- Logged Over/Under pick correct: 22/34 (65%)
- The logged pick and the replay pick differ on 4 of the 34 matches.
- Model and market disagreed on 6 matches: model right in 4, market right in 1.

Caveat: the tracker was first run part-way through the tournament and back-filled picks for games that had already been played, so for those rows the model had seen the result. The replay number is the clean one to compare with the market.
